# L1 - High-Level Requirements

Source: `AGENTS.md`, `docs/mocks/` (README assumptions 1-13), the accounts-and-profiles prompt of 18 Oct 2026 and the profile-photo prompt of 18 Oct 2026.

## L1-001: Accounts and sign-in
Only parents hold accounts. A parent creates an account by signing up, which starts a new household, or by accepting an invite from a parent already in a household. Every person, parent or reader, signs in with a username and password and chooses whether to stay signed in on the device. A parent can recover a forgotten password by e-mail.

## L1-002: Role-based access
A household holds any number of parents and readers. The system must restrict parent areas (review, books management, settings) to parents and reader areas to the signed-in household, so a reader can never reach parent functions and no household can reach another household's data.

## L1-003: Bookshelf
The system must let the reader see her books on a shelf and a book's progress, and let the grown-up add, manage and remove books, including the number of chapters and a colour-and-sticker cover.

## L1-004: Chapter logging and answers
The system must let the reader log a chapter she has read and answer the comprehension questions for it, and view her sent answers.

## L1-005: Comprehension questions
The system must give every book three default questions and let the grown-up add, edit and remove extra questions per book.

## L1-006: Review and feedback
The system must let the grown-up see chapters waiting for review, read the answers, and send a sticker (required) and a note (optional), and let the reader see that feedback.

## L1-007: Book completion and rewards
The system must recognise when a book's last chapter is logged, celebrate it for the reader, and let the grown-up give a free-text reward that appears on the reader's trophy shelf.

## L1-008: Reading streak
The system must show both users a reading streak derived from consecutive days with at least one logged chapter.

## L1-009: Household settings
The system must let a parent add and edit reader profiles (name, avatar colour, username, password), invite another parent, change their own display name, e-mail and password, and set the household appearance (match device, light, dark).

## L1-010: Feedback, errors and offline
The system must tell users the outcome of their actions (toasts and banners), show clear error pages, and keep the shelf readable and chapter logging possible while offline.

## L1-011: Responsive, delightful and accessible UI
The system must work on phones, tablets and desktops in Chromium, in light and dark themes, be fully keyboard-operable and meet WCAG 2.2 AA, and feel playful and friction-free for a 9-year-old.

## L1-012: Security and data protection
The system must protect household data against the OWASP Top 10, including credential handling, session security, input validation and per-household data isolation.

## L1-013: Performance and reliability
The system must respond quickly, never lose a submitted answer, and expose operational health and logs.

## L1-014: Production database
The system must keep every household's data in a managed PostgreSQL database that runs apart from the application host, is encrypted at rest and in transit, is backed up automatically with point-in-time restore, and whose schema changes are applied by versioned migrations. Shelfie is open to the public, so the database must hold many households at once and keep each one's data isolated and recoverable.

## L1-015: Profile photos
The system must let every person, parent or reader, add, replace and remove a photo of themselves that is shown in place of their initial wherever their avatar appears. A photo is visible only inside its household, carries no hidden location or camera data, and is erased with the household.
