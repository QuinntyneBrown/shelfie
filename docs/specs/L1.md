# L1 - High-Level Requirements

Source: `AGENTS.md`, `docs/mocks/` (README assumptions 1-13), the accounts-and-profiles prompt of 18 Oct 2026, the profile-photo prompt of 18 Oct 2026 and the admin CLI prompt of 18 Oct 2026 (L1-016, L1-017) and the book-autocomplete prompt of 18 Oct 2026 (item 2 of `docs/feature-enhancements.md`), and the author-and-volunteer-profiles prompt of 19 Oct 2026 (L1-030, L1-031).

## L1-001: Accounts and sign-in
Parents, authors and volunteers hold accounts. A parent creates an account by signing up, which starts a new household, or by accepting an invite from a parent already in a household. An independent author or a reading volunteer creates an account of their own, outside any household (L1-030, L1-031). Every person, parent, reader, author or volunteer, signs in with a username and password and chooses whether to stay signed in on the device. A parent, an author or a volunteer can recover a forgotten password by e-mail. A parent who signs up confirms their e-mail address through a single-use link Shelfie mails from `hello@shelfie.kids`; every mail Shelfie sends is branded HTML with a plain-text part.

## L1-002: Role-based access
A household holds any number of parents and readers. The system must restrict parent areas (review, books management, the directory, settings) to parents and reader areas to the signed-in household, so a reader can never reach parent functions and no household can reach another household's data. Authors and volunteers belong to no household: they reach only their own profile and catalogue, never any household, reader, book, chapter, answer, note, reward or photo, and never learn which households added their books.

## L1-003: Bookshelf
The system must let the reader see her books on a shelf and a book's progress, and let the grown-up add, manage and remove books, including the number of chapters, a colour-and-sticker cover and an optional cover picture.

## L1-004: Chapter logging and answers
The system must let the reader log a chapter she has read and answer the comprehension questions for it, and view her sent answers.

## L1-005: Comprehension questions
The system must give every book three default questions and let the grown-up add, edit and remove extra questions per book.

## L1-006: Review and feedback
The system must let the grown-up see chapters waiting for review, read the answers, and send a sticker (required) and a note (optional), and let the reader see that feedback.

## L1-007: Book completion and rewards
The system must recognise when a book's last chapter is logged, celebrate it for the reader, and let the grown-up give a free-text reward that appears on the reader's trophy shelf. It must also give each finished book a printable certificate naming the reader, the book and the day she finished it.

## L1-008: Reading streak
The system must show both users a reading streak derived from consecutive days with at least one logged chapter.

## L1-009: Household settings
The system must let a parent add and edit reader profiles (name, avatar colour, username, password), invite another parent, change their own display name, e-mail and password, and set the household appearance (match device, light, dark).

## L1-010: Feedback, errors and offline
The system must tell users the outcome of their actions (toasts and banners), show clear error pages, and keep the shelf readable and chapter logging possible while offline.

## L1-011: Responsive, delightful and accessible UI
The system must work on phones, tablets and desktops in Chromium, in light and dark themes, be fully keyboard-operable and meet WCAG 2.2 AA, and feel playful and friction-free for a 9-year-old.

## L1-012: Security and data protection
The system must protect household data against the OWASP Top 10, including credential handling, session security, input validation and per-household data isolation.

## L1-013: Performance and reliability
The system must respond quickly, never lose a submitted answer, and expose operational health and logs.

## L1-014: Production database
The system must keep every household's data in a managed PostgreSQL database that runs apart from the application host, is encrypted at rest and in transit, is backed up automatically with point-in-time restore, and whose schema changes are applied by versioned migrations. Shelfie is open to the public, so the database must hold many households at once and keep each one's data isolated and recoverable.

## L1-015: Profile photos
The system must let every person, parent or reader, add, replace and remove a photo of themselves that is shown in place of their initial wherever their avatar appears. A photo is visible only inside its household, carries no hidden location or camera data, and is erased with the household.

## L1-016: Operator administration CLI
The operator (the parent who runs the production service) must be able to query and change production data from a terminal with a command-line tool, `shelfie-admin`, installed as a .NET global tool. The tool reaches production only through a dedicated admin HTTP API, never through the database, the VM or SSH. It must be safe by default for changes and give both readable and machine-readable output.

## L1-017: Admin API security
The admin API must use a credential dedicated to the CLI, separate from web sign-in: scoped, expiring and revocable admin tokens, granted with least privilege. Every admin call must be rate limited and audit logged on the server, and no secret may ever be printed or stored in plain text.

## L1-018: AI review assistant
The system must let a parent, on request, ask an assistant to read one pending chapter's questions and answers, point out answers that show weak understanding with a short reason, and draft a short, warm note the parent can edit before sending. The parent stays in charge: the assistant never picks the sticker or sends anything, its suggestions are never stored or shown to the reader, only the chapter's book title, number, questions and answers leave Shelfie, and reviewing works the same without it.

## L1-019: AI-assisted questions
The system must let a parent ask for suggested comprehension questions for a book, drafted by an AI service from the book's title, author, next chapter number and existing questions only. The parent reads, edits or discards each suggestion and saves only the ones they choose, through the same rules as questions they write themselves. Nothing the AI writes reaches a reader until a parent saves it, readers never see or trigger the feature, and adding questions by hand keeps working whenever the AI service is missing, slow or failing.
## L1-020: Book autocomplete
The system must help whoever adds a book find it by typing its title: a short list of matching books appears, and picking one fills in the author, the chapter count where a figure is known and a cover picture, all of which stay editable before the book is saved. Matches come from a public book catalogue, with an AI helper as a labelled fallback when the catalogue has nothing. Only the typed title ever leaves the server, the browser never calls a third party, and adding a book by hand works exactly as before when the lookup is unavailable.

## L1-021: Reward gallery
The system must let a parent keep a gallery of rewards, each with a name, the number of finished books that earns it, an optional description and optional pictures, one of which is featured, and let the reader pick one as the goal she is reading toward and watch her progress to it. Progress counts the books she finishes from the moment she picks the goal. When she reaches it she sees a celebration, and the parent sees it is ready to give and gives it through the usual Give a reward flow. The reader sees each reward's featured picture and description but cannot change them. Free-text rewards per finished book and the trophy shelf work exactly as before; only the household's parents can change the gallery.

## L1-022: Cover photo picker
The system must let whoever adds a book, after picking a match from the title lookup, browse the other cover pictures the public book catalogue has for that book and pick one as the book's cover. The pictures come only from the catalogue, through the Shelfie API: the browser never calls a third party, and the chosen picture goes through the same checks as any other cover. Choosing is optional, the match's own cover stays the default, and adding a book works exactly as before when no other covers are available.

## L1-023: Voice answers
The system must let a reader dictate an answer to a comprehension question instead of typing it. The words she speaks are turned into text by her browser, appended to the answer where she can read and edit them, and sent only when she logs the chapter like any typed answer. Shelfie never records, receives or stores audio, and typing works exactly as before when dictation is unavailable or refused.

## L1-024: Achievement badges
The system must give the reader badges for reading feats: finishing her first book, reading two chapters in one day, reading on a weekend, and reading seven days in a row. Her shelf shows the badges she has earned with the day she earned each, and the ones still to earn with a hint of how, and the parent sees the same badges on Review. Badges are worked out from her chapter logs and finished books, never entered or edited by hand, and they belong to the household like the streak.

## L1-025: Streak rewards
The system must let a parent set a reward for each of three reading-streak milestones: 7, 30 and 100 days in a row. When the household's streak reaches a milestone that has a reward, the reward is earned and stays earned even if the streak later breaks. The reader sees on her shelf how close the next milestone is and celebrates when she earns one; the parent sees on Review that it is ready and gives it with one tap, and it then shows on her shelf as given. A reward can carry an amount of money, and the reader's shelf shows each reward as a badge, a shield with its milestone and a coin with its amount, at the end of a path of days. Each milestone is earned and given once. Only the household's parents set the rewards, and the free-text reward per finished book, the reward gallery and the streak itself work exactly as before.

## L1-026: In-app feedback
The system must let anyone signed in, a reader or a parent, send feedback without leaving Shelfie: a score from 1 to 5 for how Shelfie feels to use, whether it is about how it feels or an idea, and a short message, of which at least a score or a message is given. Feedback is saved against the sender's own household and profile, is never shown to another household, and is erased with the household. The operator reads it, newest first, through the admin API and the `shelfie-admin` CLI; there is no screen inside Shelfie for reading or answering it.

## L1-027: Feedback notifications
The system must be able to send a push notification to a browser, when its owner has turned that on, to tell a parent that a chapter is waiting for feedback and to tell a reader that a sticker, a note or a reward has arrived. Each person turns notifications on for the device in front of them and can turn them off again; a parent also chooses, for the whole household, which of the four events send one: a chapter logged, a sticker or note sent, a book reward given and a streak reward given. All four start on. A notification is one short line that opens the page it is about, never carries a reader's answers, and is sent only to the household it belongs to. Sending is optional: where the server has no push keys, nothing is sent and nothing is offered. The in-app toasts and banners, and every screen's behaviour with notifications off, work exactly as before; there is no notification centre and no e-mail.
## L1-028: Marketing site
The system must have a public marketing page at `https://www.shelfie.kids` that tells families what Shelfie is and how its reading loop works (read a chapter, log it, answer the questions, a parent reviews, a reward for finishing the book), shows streaks, badges, the reward gallery and voice answers with real pictures of the app, and sends visitors to the app to sign up or sign in. The page is static, works on phones, tablets and desktops in light and dark, is keyboard-operable, respects reduced motion, loads no third-party scripts and carries no analytics. The app and its API move to `https://app.shelfie.kids` unchanged, and the bare `shelfie.kids` keeps old links working by redirecting.

## L1-029: Household set-up wizard
The system must take a parent who has just signed up through a one-time set-up wizard, one purpose per step: add their child, choose how the child signs in, and create a reward to read toward, ending with the child's sign-in details shown once. Each step is minimal: one question, one answer, one button, with a step indicator and a way to skip. The wizard runs once per household, on whichever device the parent uses, and never shows again after it is finished or skipped; parents who join an existing household by invite and households that existed before the wizard never see it. It reuses the same reader and reward rules as Settings, where everything it creates can be changed afterwards.

## L1-030: Author profiles and catalogues
The system must let an independent children's author sign up for an account of their own, outside any household, write a profile (a display name, a short bio, an optional photo and an optional website link) and keep a catalogue of their books, each with a title, a chapter count, an optional cover picture and a short description. Parents browse approved authors in a directory, open an author's profile and catalogue, and add a catalogue book to their household's shelf through the usual Add a book flow, filled in from the catalogue entry; the book then belongs to the household like any other. A new profile stays hidden from parents until the operator approves it, and the operator can hide it again. Readers never see authors, profiles or catalogues; an author never reaches a household or learns who added their books. Sign-up is free; paid profiles are deferred (ADR-0021).

## L1-031: Volunteer profiles
The system must let a person who wants to help children with reading sign up for an account of their own, outside any household, and write a profile: a display name, a short bio, an optional photo, the ages they help with and how a parent can reach them outside Shelfie. Parents browse approved volunteers in the same directory and read the contact line; Shelfie sends nothing between them, does not vet volunteers, and says so. A new profile stays hidden from parents until the operator approves it, and the operator can hide it again. Readers never see volunteers, and a volunteer never reaches a household.
