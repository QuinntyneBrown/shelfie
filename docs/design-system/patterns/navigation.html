Pattern · Navigation

Navigation and page structure

Nia has one place to be, so her shell has no navigation at all: brand, streak, avatar. Dad has three areas, reached from the top bar on tablets and desktops and from a bottom tab bar on phones. Every page below the top level has a breadcrumb, and that is the way back.

Overview

Shelfie's structure is deliberately shallow. The reader's world is the shelf and the pages under one book; the grown-up's world is Review, Books and Settings. There are no tabs, no sidebar and no sub-navigation.

  • Sign-in shell: no top bar. A centred card (.auth-layout + .auth-card) under the Shelfie sticker. Sign in, sign up, accept invite, forgot password, reset password.
  • Reader shell: top bar with the brand (links to the shelf), the streak and Nia's avatar, which opens the account menu. Nothing else.
  • Grown-up shell: top bar with the brand (links to Review), Review / Books / Settings as nav links from 768px, and Dad's avatar. Below 768px the links move to a bottom tab bar.
  • Page header: every page starts with .page-header: the <h1>, a one-line subtitle that summarises the page's state, and at most one action.
  • Breadcrumb: every page that is not a top-level page shows a breadcrumb above the page header.

Sign-in shell

Welcome back

pages/sign-in/default. No top bar, no navigation. Everyone, parents and readers, signs in on this one form.

Back behaviour

  • The breadcrumb is the way back. There is no back-arrow button in the top bar. The parent crumb is always a link; the current page is plain text with aria-current="page".
  • Forms offer a way out next to the primary button that goes to the parent page: "Not yet" on log a chapter, "Cancel" on add a book. A draft is kept (see forms).
  • Finishing a task offers the way home first: "Back to my shelf" on Nia's celebrations, "Back to Review" after feedback is sent, with the next task as a second choice.
  • The browser's back button always works. Every page and every state a person can link to has its own address; dialogs and the account menu do not change the address.
  • The brand goes home: the shelf for Nia, Review for Dad.

Responsive behaviour

WidthReaderGrown-up

768px and upSameNav links in the top bar (.topbar__nav); tab bar hidden; .has-tabbar padding removed
  • The top bar is sticky at every width and 56px tall; the page scrolls under it, and scroll-padding-top keeps focused elements clear of it.
  • The tab bar respects the phone's safe area at the bottom.
  • Breadcrumbs wrap rather than truncate, so long book titles stay readable.
  • The page header wraps its action under the title on phones.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Landmarks: <header class="topbar">, <main id="main">, <nav aria-label="Primary"> for the top-bar links, <nav aria-label="Primary, phone"> for the tab bar (only one is visible at a time) and <nav aria-label="Breadcrumb"> around an <ol>. The avatar is a menu button (WAI-ARIA APG Menu Button). A skip link to #main is the first focusable element on every page.

Keyboard

Focus

After a route change focus goes to the new page's <h1> so the title is read. The shared focus ring shows on nav links, tabs, crumbs and the avatar.

Labelling

The streak has aria-label="5-day reading streak" because "5 days" alone is unclear. The Review count has aria-label="2 chapters to review". The avatar names whose menu it is: "Account menu for Nia". Tab-bar icons are aria-hidden; the text label is the name.

Motion

Nav links fade their background over --duration-fast; nothing slides. The skip link drops in over the same duration. Reduced motion removes both.

Touch

Nav links and tabs are at least 44px tall; each tab takes a third of the bar. The avatar button is 32px, above the 24px minimum, with nothing else tappable beside it.

Content

  • Area names are one word: Review, Books, Settings. Nia's home is "My shelf" in breadcrumbs.
  • Page titles greet or name: "Hi Nia 👋" on the shelf; the book's title on a book page; "Chapter 12" on the log form; "Review", "Books", "Settings" for Dad.
  • Subtitles summarise state with middots: "🔥 5-day streak · 2 books on the go · 1 reward waiting"; "5 books · 2 on the go · 2 finished · 1 up next".
  • Crumbs use the page's title, so the last crumb matches the <h1>: "Chapter 12", "Chapter 11 · The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe".
