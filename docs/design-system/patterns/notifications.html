Pattern · Feedback

Notifications

A toast reports something that just happened and then gets out of the way. A banner reports something that is still true, or news that is waiting, and stays until it is no longer true or is dismissed. A push reaches the person when Shelfie is closed.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stablePattern

Overview

Shelfie has two in-app notification surfaces and one that arrives from the operating system. It has no notification centre and no e-mail.

  • Toast: dark, floating, bottom-right (bottom of the screen on phones). Short-lived results: "Chapter 12 saved", "Dad added a book", "Back online", "Couldn’t save your answers". Often has one action: Undo, Retry, See it, Review Chapter 10.
  • Banner (.alert.banner): full-width strip under the top bar that pushes the page down. Lasting conditions ("No internet connection", "Shelfie’s server isn’t answering"), news for Nia ("Dad read Chapter 9 and gave you a Star ⭐", "Dad gave you a reward for Matilda") and Dad's waiting work ("2 chapters are waiting for your feedback").
  • Push (the operating system's own notification): arrives when Shelfie is closed or in the background, and is built by the browser, not by a component. See Push.

For feedback tied to a field, a form or a region, see feedback and loading; notifications are for things that are not attached to what the person is looking at.

When to use

QuestionToastBanner
Is it still true after the person moves on?No: an eventYes: a condition or waiting news
Is it about the action they just took?UsuallyRarely
Can it be undone or retried right now?Yes, with one toast actionOnly a retry for a lasting failure ("Try now")
Does it span pages?No; it dies with its timer or on dismissYes; offline and server banners follow the person

Priority

Tone decides how loud a notification is and whether it waits for the person.

ToneMeansToast exampleBanner exampleAnnounced
DangerSomething failed and needs actionCouldn’t save your answers · RetryShelfie’s server isn’t answering · Try nowAssertive (role="alert")
WarningSomething is limited but safeYou’re offline · saved on this deviceNo internet connectionPolite
SuccessSomething worked or something good arrivedChapter 12 saved · Undo🏆 Dad gave you a reward for MatildaPolite
InfoSomething changed that is worth knowingDad added a book · See itDad read Chapter 9 and gave you a Star ⭐Polite
  • One banner at a time. If two apply, the higher tone shows: a connection banner replaces news; news waits until the connection is back.
  • Lasting conditions turn into a toast when they end. The offline banner stays on every page until the connection returns, then becomes the "Back online" toast.

Auto-dismiss rules

KindDismisses
Info and success toastsAfter 8 seconds, never less. A thin bar along the bottom shows the time left. The timer pauses while the pointer is over the toast or focus is inside it, and starts again from where it stopped.
Toast with UndoSame 8 seconds or more; Undo works for exactly as long as the toast is on screen.
Warning toastsStay until dismissed; no timer bar.
Danger toastsStay until dismissed or until the retry succeeds; no timer bar.
News banners (feedback, reward)Stay until dismissed with the close button, or until Nia opens what they point to.
Condition banners (offline, server, chapters waiting)Stay while the condition is true; no close button.

Push

A push says something is waiting while the person is somewhere else. It is the same news as a banner, delivered earlier. When Shelfie is open the person already sees the toast or banner, so a push is for the closed app.

EventGoes toTitleBodyOpensReplaces (tag)
Chapter loggedParentsNia read Chapter 9 of MatildaReady for your feedback.Review of that chapterchapter-<id>
Sticker or note sentReadersDad read Chapter 9 and gave you a Star 🌟See what he said.That chapterfeedback-<id>
Book reward givenReaders🏆 Dad gave you a reward for MatildaCome and see what it is.Her shelfreward-<book>
Streak reward givenReaders🔥 7 days in a row! Dad gave you a rewardCome and see what it is.Her shelfstreak-<milestone>
  • Ask only after a tap. The browser's permission prompt appears only after the person taps Turn on (the shelf banner for readers, the "Notify this device" switch in Settings for parents). Never on page load.
  • Blocked is explained, not nagged. If the browser has blocked notifications, the switch is disabled and the card says where to allow them. If the server cannot send pushes, the card says so and the shelf banner never shows.
  • One line each, no noise. No sound, no badge count and no more than one notification per tag: a second one about the same thing replaces the first.
  • Copy is the in-app copy. Title and body use the words of the matching banner, so Nia hears the same voice in both places.

Mocks: chapter-logged, feedback, book-reward, streak-reward; shelf offer, Settings card.

Structure

Stacked toasts

Toasts live in one region. The newest enters at the bottom and older ones slide up. At most three are visible; when a fourth arrives the oldest collapses into a "+1 more" line at the top of the region.

Responsive behaviour

  • Toasts: from 640px the region is 24rem wide in the bottom-right corner; on phones it spans the bottom of the screen with a 16px margin. While the grown-up tab bar is showing (under 768px), components.css lifts the region above it (body:has(.tabbar)), so a toast never covers navigation.
  • Banners: full width under the top bar at every size. On phones the text and action stack; from 640px they sit on one centred row.
  • Banners push content down rather than overlapping it, so nothing under them is hidden.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The toast region is a polite live region (role="status" aria-live="polite") present on every page from the start, so new toasts are announced. A danger toast adds role="alert". Banners are role="status", except the server-down banner, which is role="alert". There is no WAI-ARIA APG pattern for toasts; this follows the status-message rules of WCAG 4.1.3.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabToast and banner actions and close buttons are in the normal tab order after the page content they relate to; reaching one pauses the toast's timer.
Enter / SpaceRuns the action (Undo, Retry, See it) or dismisses.

Focus

A toast never takes focus when it appears. When a focused toast is dismissed or times out, focus returns to the element that was focused before it. The 8-second minimum and the pause on hover and focus meet WCAG 2.2.1 (timing adjustable).

Labelling

Close buttons say "Dismiss". Icons are aria-hidden; the title carries the meaning, so tone is never colour or icon alone. The timer bar is aria-hidden.

Motion

Toasts slide up and fade in over --duration-base and fade down on leaving; older toasts slide up to make room. The timer bar shrinks linearly. Under reduced motion toasts appear and disappear without movement; the timer still runs.

Touch

Close buttons are 24px targets with space around them; toast actions are text buttons with padding to reach 24px. Toasts never cover the tab bar or a form's submit button.

Content

  • Title: what happened, in as few words as possible: "Chapter 12 saved", "Sent to Nia", "Back online".
  • Text: what it means now: "5 to go. Dad will read your answers soon."; "Chapter 11 is reviewed. Chapter 10 is still waiting."
  • One action, a verb: "Undo", "Retry", "See it", "Try now", or a destination: "Review Chapter 10", "Open Chapter 9".
  • Failures reassure: "They’re still on the page."; "We saved Chapter 12 on this device and will send it when you’re back online."
  • Good news for Nia sounds like Dad: "So proud of you for sticking with a long one!"
