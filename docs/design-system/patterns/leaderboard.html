Pattern

Leaderboard

How a household reading challenge shows who is ahead: ranked rows with avatar, count and progress, the leader marked, insights under the board, and a result when it ends.

When to use it

  • Only for a challenge Dad started, and only inside one household. Never compare households.
  • Two or more readers taking part: a Leaderboard. One reader: a large progress bar toward the goal and one line saying how many are left.

Structure

  1. Card heading: the challenge name. Subtitle: what counts, the goal, the dates or days left.
  2. The leaderboard, highest count first; equal counts share a rank, and at the top they share the crown.
  3. On Review only: up to three insight lines (.insights), one per line, each led by an emoji: 🏅 top reader this week, 📈 biggest climber since last week, 🔥 longest streak. A line with nothing to say is left out.
  4. Actions: End now while it runs; Give a reward and Remove once it has ended.
  1. 👑N
    Nia In the lead
    9 of 15
  2. 2T
    Theo
    6 of 15
  • 🏅 Nia read the most this week: 6 chapters
  • 📈 Theo climbed the most: 3 more chapters than last week
  • 🔥 Nia has the longest streak: 5 days
The Review card, from review/challenges.

Ending and winning

  • The winner is the reader with the most at the end; a tie has several winners; nobody wins when nothing was logged. A single reader wins by reaching the goal.
  • On the winner's shelf the card is a Celebration. A reader who did not win gets one warm line about what she did.
  • An ended challenge stays on the shelf for 7 days; Review keeps it under Past challenges until Dad removes it.

Tone

Friendly competition between siblings: say how many to catch up ("3 chapters to catch Theo"), never how far behind or who is last.
