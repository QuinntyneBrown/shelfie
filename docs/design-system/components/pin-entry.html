PIN entry

Nia's way in: four dots and a big round keypad. No typing, no submit button: the PIN checks itself when the fourth digit lands.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableNative <button> keypad

Overview

PIN entry is two parts on the reader's sign-in card: .pin-dots, four dots that fill as digits are pressed, and .pinpad, a 3 × 4 grid of buttons. It is used in exactly one place: after Nia taps her profile tile.

  • The PIN submits itself on the fourth digit. There is no "Sign in" button to find.
  • A wrong PIN clears the dots, turns them red and says how many tries are left.
  • Five wrong tries lock the keypad for five minutes with a calm explanation.

Dad sets the PIN in Settings with an ordinary text field. Dad signs in with a password, never a PIN.

Anatomy

  1. PIN dotsFour 16px circles, thick --color-border-strong ring; filled with --color-accent via data-filled. role="status".
  2. Digit keyA secondary .btn, round, 56px, --font-size-2xl semibold.
  3. "Not Nia?"A ghost link back to the profile picker, in the bottom-left slot.
  4. Delete keyGhost icon button, aria-label="Delete last digit".

Responsive behaviour

  • The keypad is 16rem wide at most and centred in the 26rem auth card, so it looks and feels the same on a phone, a tablet and a laptop.
  • At 360px the card fills the width with --layout-margin on each side; the keypad keeps its three columns.
  • Keys stay 56px tall at every width: big enough for a nine-year-old's thumb on a phone held in one hand.

Theming

Keys are ordinary .btns re-sized through the button's component tokens inside .pinpad (--btn-height, --btn-font-size) plus a full radius. Dots read --color-border-strong empty, --color-accent filled and --color-danger-solid when the PIN was wrong. Both themes come from those semantic tokens.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Native buttons in a role="group" named "PIN keypad". The dots are a role="status" live region whose aria-label is updated ("2 of 4 digits entered"), so each press is announced without reading the digits aloud. The wrong-PIN message is joined with aria-describedby and the dots carry aria-invalid="true".

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the keys in reading order: 1 to 9, Not Nia?, 0, Delete.
Enter, SpacePresses the focused key.

Focus

The shared ring on the focused key. After a wrong PIN, focus stays on the key that was last pressed so Nia can start again without hunting. When the keypad locks, focus moves to the "Not Nia?" link, the one control still available.

Labelling

Digit keys are named by their digit. The delete key has aria-label="Delete last digit". The greeting ("Hi Nia!") is the page's h1; the instruction "Tap your 4-digit PIN." sits directly above the dots.

Filled versus empty is shown by fill, not only by colour, and a wrong PIN also shows text.

Motion

Dots fill over --duration-fast. The sign-in mock notes a single 120ms shake of the dots on a wrong PIN; it is not in components.css, and if built it must be removed under prefers-reduced-motion.

Touch

56px round keys with --space-3 (12px) between them: well above the 44px comfortable target.

Authentication

Remembering a PIN counts as a cognitive function test under WCAG 3.3.8, and a keypad cannot be filled by a password manager. The only alternative today is the grown-up: Dad signs in with his password and can reset the PIN in Settings, and the lockout message says so: "Ask Dad if you’ve forgotten it." Treat this as a known gap to review before release.

Content

  • Greet by name and give one instruction: "Hi Nia!", "Tap your 4-digit PIN."
  • A wrong PIN is a fact plus what is left: "That PIN didn’t match. 3 tries left." No "Wrong!" and no "Invalid PIN".
  • The lockout is a pause, not a punishment: "Let’s take a short break", then why, how long, and who can help.
  • "Not Nia?" is the way back, phrased as the question she would ask.
