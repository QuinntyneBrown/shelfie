Profile tile

Large tiles that answer "Who’s reading today?". Nia taps her own face and name to open her shelf, with no typing; Dad taps his to go to the password screen.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableNative <button>

Overview

Use profile tiles only on the sign-in picker. A household has exactly one reader and one grown-up, so there are always exactly two tiles. Picking a tile does not sign anyone in: Nia's tile leads to her PIN entry, Dad's to the password form.

  • The tiles sit in a .profiles row, centred, inside the sign-in card.
  • Nia's tile comes first and takes autofocus, because she is the one who opens Shelfie most.
  • For switching person once signed in, use the account menu, not tiles.

Anatomy

  1. Avatar64px avatar with initials in the person's colour (rose for Nia, sky for Dad). Decorative.
  2. Name"Nia" or "Dad", in the h4 font. The accessible name.
  3. Role"Reader" or "Grown-up" in a muted <small>.
  4. ContainerNative button, min 8rem wide, 24 × 16px padding, hairline border, --radius-lg.

Variants

One tile style. The avatar colour is the only difference between the two people.

The sign-in picker: Nia in rose, Dad (Marcus Bell) in sky.
HTML
<div class="profiles">
  <button class="profile-tile" type="button" autofocus>
    <span class="avatar avatar--rose" aria-hidden="true">N</span>Nia<small>Reader</small>
  </button>
  <button class="profile-tile" type="button">
    <span class="avatar avatar--sky" aria-hidden="true">MB</span>Dad<small>Grown-up</small>
  </button>
</div>

Sizes

One size, deliberately large: about 128 × 150px with a 64px avatar, so a 9-year-old can hit it first time.

States

Pointer and focus states are forced with data-state here; real pages get them from :hover, :focus-visible and :active. There is no disabled or loading state: both people can always be picked, and the next screen loads instantly.

Both themes

  • Hover lifts the tile 2px with a springy ease, deepens the shadow and darkens the border.
  • Active settles it back down, so a tap feels like pressing a real button.
  • Focus is the shared 2px ring with a 2px gap.

Responsive behaviour

  • The two tiles sit side by side from 360px: 2 × 8rem plus a 24px gap is 280px, inside the 26rem sign-in card.
  • .profiles wraps and centres; on a very narrow or zoomed screen (400%) Dad's tile drops below Nia's.
  • Tiles never stretch to fill a wide screen; the sign-in card caps the width.

Theming

The tile reads --color-bg-surface, --color-border-default (hover --color-border-strong), --color-fg-default and --color-fg-muted; shadows are --shadow-1/--shadow-2. Avatar colours come from --color-cover-rose and --color-cover-sky with --color-fg-on-cover initials, which are tuned per theme. In forced-colours mode the tile gets a system-colour border.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Native <button type="button">; the two tiles are a plain row of buttons, not a radio group, because picking one navigates.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves between Nia and Dad.
Enter, SpacePicks that person and opens the PIN or password step.

Focus

Nia's tile has autofocus on load, so a keyboard user can press Enter at once. When someone comes back with "Not Nia?" or "Not Dad?", focus lands on the tile they came from.

Labelling

The button's name is its text: "Nia Reader", "Dad Grown-up". Hide the initials with aria-hidden="true" on the avatar, otherwise the name is read as "N Nia Reader".

Motion

The 2px lift uses --duration-fast with --ease-spring. Under prefers-reduced-motion the duration is zeroed, so the tile changes shadow without moving.

Touch

Each tile is roughly 128 × 150px, far above the 44px comfortable target, with 24px between tiles. Hover is a bonus; nothing depends on it.

Content

  • Name: what the reader calls the person. "Dad", not "Marcus Bell"; "Nia", the name set in Settings.
  • Role: one word, "Reader" or "Grown-up".
  • Heading above the tiles: "Who’s reading today?", with "Pick your name to open your shelf." under it.
