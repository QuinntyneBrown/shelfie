Chat bubble

Chapter questions as a little chat. Olive the owl asks in a bubble, Nia answers in a bubble that is the textarea itself, and a Next button moves her on. It feels like talking about the book, not filling in a worksheet.

Overview

Use the chat for the comprehension questions on Log a chapter, and only there. It is a form dressed as a conversation: every question is a real <label> and every answer a real textarea, so it submits, validates and reads to assistive technology like any other form.

  • Buddy bubble (.bubble--buddy): Olive's face and a speech bubble. A welcome or a short reply is a p.bubble__text; a question is a label.bubble__text pointing at the answer below it.
  • Answer bubble (.bubble--me): the textarea, indented past Olive's face, with a small "Speak your answer" mic and a Next button. Next stays disabled until there is text.
  • Hint (.chat__hint): how many questions are left.

Once answers are sent they are played back with Question and answer, not the chat. For Dad's notes back to Nia use the feedback note.

Anatomy

  1. Olive's faceimg.bubble__face, ../assets/olive.svg, --size-avatar-md (40px), alt="": decorative, she is a character, not information.
  2. Speech bubblep.bubble__text: --color-accent-subtle, --radius-lg with a small --radius-xs tail corner at the bottom left, --text-body.
  3. Question bubblelabel.bubble__text for="q1" with a .qa__num number; tapping it focuses the answer.
  4. Answer bubble.bubble--me.field holding textarea.textarea; --radius-lg with the tail corner at the bottom right; indented by the face width plus --space-3.
  5. MicGhost small button, "Speak your answer", aria-pressed.
  6. NextSecondary small button, disabled until the answer has text; reveals the next question.
  7. Hintp.chat__hint, --text-body-sm in --color-fg-subtle, aligned with the bubbles.

Variants

Three bubbles make up the chat. Olive speaks (welcome or reply), Olive asks (a question, which is a label), Nia answers. Olive's replies are canned and short ("Nice! I wondered about that too.", "Good pick!"); they never judge the answer.

Sizes

One size. Bubbles are at most 40rem wide, Olive's face is --size-avatar-md (40px) and the answer box is the standard textarea (three rows, grows by drag). The mic and Next are small buttons (36px).

States

States live on the answer bubble; Olive's bubbles never change. Each row uses the real attribute: disabled on Next, aria-pressed on the mic, aria-invalid and aria-describedby on the textarea, readonly while sending, aria-busy while loading. Focus is shown with data-state="focus".

  • Dictating: the mic is pressed and shows a pulsing .mic-dot; "Listening… tap to stop" sits beside it; words appear in the box and stay editable.
  • Mic denied: the typed answer stays and the message sits under the box; she can tap Speak your answer again.
  • Invalid: only question 1 is required. The message is kind and says what to do.
  • Read-only (submitting): after "I'm done!" every answer is readonly and the mic and Next go away while the form sends.

Responsive behaviour

  • Mobile first: the chat fills the card. Bubbles grow to their text and stop at 40rem, so lines stay readable on a desktop.
  • The answer bubble keeps its indent (face width plus --space-3) at every width, which lines it up under Olive's words. At 320px that still leaves about 220px for typing.
  • The mic and Next sit right-aligned under the box in a wrapping cluster; on a phone Next drops under the mic, and "Listening… tap to stop" wraps under them.

Theming

Olive's bubbles read --color-accent-subtle with --color-fg-default text and --color-fg-accent numbers; the answer bubble is the textarea, so it reads the field tokens (--color-bg-surface, --color-border-strong, focus and danger). All of them flip with the theme. Olive herself is a fixed illustration that reads on both the light and dark card. To re-theme, override --color-accent-subtle or the field component tokens on the chat; never colour a bubble inline.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

It is a form, not a chat log. The chat is a <fieldset> whose <legend> ("Tell Dad about it") names the group. Each question is a native <label for> on its textarea; the mic is a toggle button (WAI-ARIA button pattern, aria-pressed). Olive's welcome and replies are plain paragraphs: no role="log", no live region, so a screen reader is not interrupted each time she speaks. Olive's face is alt="".

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabAnswer, then Speak your answer, then Next (when enabled), then the next answer.
Enter in the answerNew line. It never submits or moves on.
Enter / Space on the micStarts or stops listening.
Enter / Space on NextShows Olive's reply and the next question, and moves focus into the new answer.

Focus

The answer shows the shared focus ring and border, like any textarea. Next moves focus to the new answer box, so the new question label is read with it. Clicking a question bubble focuses its answer.

Labelling

The accessible name of each answer is its question, number included ("1. What happened in this chapter?"). The mic and Next buttons repeat the question number in aria-label ("Speak your answer to question 1", "Next question after question 1") because several are on the page. Error and mic messages are tied in with aria-describedby. "Listening" and "Stopped listening" are announced once through the page's polite status region, not by the bubbles.

Motion

No typing dots or sliding bubbles: a new question simply appears. The only motion is the mic's pulsing dot, which stops under prefers-reduced-motion.

Touch

The whole question bubble is a label, so it is a large tap target for the answer. The mic and Next are 36px tall with --space-2 between them, above the 24px minimum.

Content

  • Olive talks to Nia by name, in short, warm sentences: "Hi Nia! Dad wants to hear about Chapter 12. A sentence or two is perfect, and spelling doesn’t matter."
  • Questions are numbered and end with a question mark. Dad's own questions appear word for word.
  • Replies are one short line of encouragement that fits any answer ("Nice! I wondered about that too.", "Good pick!", "Ooh, we’ll find out soon."). Never "Correct" or "Wrong".
  • Placeholder: "Type here, or tap the mic…". Buttons: "Speak your answer", "Next".
  • Hint: "3 more questions after this one."; drop it on the last question.
