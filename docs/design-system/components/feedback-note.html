Feedback note

The sticker and short note Dad sends after reading a chapter. It sits at the top of the chapter page, so it is the first thing Nia sees when she opens it.

Overview

Use the feedback note only for feedback Dad has already sent on a chapter. A sticker is required to send feedback and the note is optional, so a feedback note always has a sticker and sometimes has no text.

  • On the chapter page (Nia) it says who it is from: "Dad gave you a Star".
  • On a reviewed chapter (Dad) it says what he sent and whether Nia has seen it: "You gave a Star".
  • Before Dad has read the chapter there is no feedback note; the chapter page shows an info alert, "Dad hasn’t read this yet", in its place.
  • The banner on her shelf ("Dad read Chapter 9 and gave you a Star") is an alert that links here, not a feedback note.

Anatomy

  1. StickerSticker at .sticker--lg (64px), decorative, aria-hidden.
  2. From line.feedback__from: who and which sticker, in words. It names the section.
  3. Note (optional)A plain <p> in body text. Left out when Dad wrote nothing.
  4. Meta.feedback__meta: when it was sent; on Dad's side, when Nia saw it.
  5. ContainerAccent-subtle fill, 30% accent hairline, --radius-lg, 16px padding.

Variants

One look, two points of view. The from line and meta change with who is reading.

🌟
Dad gave you a Star

Great details, Nia! I love that you noticed Edmund felt scared in the Witch’s house. Keep going!

Wed 14 Oct, 8:40 pm
🌟
You gave a Star

Great details, Nia! I love that you noticed Edmund felt scared in the Witch’s house. Keep going!

Wed 14 Oct, 8:40 pm · Nia saw it Thu 15 Oct
Top: Nia on the chapter page. Bottom: Dad on a reviewed chapter.
HTML
<section class="feedback" aria-labelledby="from-dad">
  <span class="sticker sticker--lg" aria-hidden="true">🌟</span>
  <div class="feedback__body">
    <span class="feedback__from" id="from-dad">Dad gave you a Star</span>
    <p>Great details, Nia! …</p>
    <span class="feedback__meta">Wed 14 Oct, 8:40 pm</span>
  </div>
</section>

Sizes

One size. It is as wide as the page column and as tall as the note.

States

The note is not interactive, so there is no hover, focus, active or disabled state. Its states are about content.

Responsive behaviour

  • Sticker and text stay side by side at every width; the 64px sticker leaves about 230px for text at 360px, which fits a short note.
  • The note wraps; the body has min-width: 0 so a long word cannot push the box wider than the screen.
  • The note sits full width above the answers card on every breakpoint, so Nia sees it before scrolling.

Theming

The container reads --color-accent-subtle and a hairline mixed from --color-accent at 30%; text reads --color-fg-accent (from line), --color-fg-default (note) and --color-fg-muted (meta). In the dark theme the accent-subtle fill turns deep violet and the accent text lightens, so all three text pairs keep 4.5:1. The sticker background is the same accent-subtle, so it blends into the box by design.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A native <section> with aria-labelledby pointing at the from line, which makes it a named region ("Dad gave you a Star") that screen-reader users can jump to.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabNot a stop; nothing inside is interactive.
Region navigation (screen reader)Jumps to the note by its name.

Focus

No focus. The page's first stop after the breadcrumb is the next control below the answers.

Labelling

The sticker is aria-hidden; its meaning is in the from line as a word ("a Star"), never only as an emoji. Give each note a unique id for aria-labelledby.

Contrast

From line on fill
Note on fill
Meta on fill

The 30% accent border is decorative; the fill and the from line carry the meaning.

Motion

None.

Touch

No targets.

Content

  • From line: "Dad gave you a {sticker}" for Nia, "You gave a {sticker}" for Dad. Use the sticker's name from the picker: Star, Wise owl, Rocket, Dragon, Cookie, Bright idea.
  • The note is Dad's words as typed. The picker's help text asks him to keep it short and warm, because she reads it on her own.
  • Meta uses the short date form: "Wed 14 Oct, 8:40 pm". Dad's side adds "· Nia saw it Thu 15 Oct" once she has opened it.
