Form layout

The parts that hold a form together: the row of actions at the end, the error summary at the top, the danger zone below, and what everything does while it saves.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableNative <form>

Overview

Form layout is three small classes plus one rule for the submitting state. The whole composition (field order, cards, validation timing) is in the forms pattern.

  • .form-actions ends every form: an end-aligned, wrapping row with the primary last.
  • .form-summary is an alert (.alert.alert--danger) at the top of a form that failed. It counts the problems and links each to its field.
  • .danger-zone sits after the form on Manage book. It holds the one destructive action and says what it deletes; the button opens a confirmation dialog.
  • Submitting: the pressed button gets aria-busy="true", text fields become readonly, radios, swatches and chips become disabled.

Anatomy

  1. Error summary.alert.alert--danger.form-summary, role="alert", tabindex="-1"; a thicker --color-danger-solid left edge.
  2. Error listAn <ol> of links, one per problem, each to its field's #id; indented by --space-5.
  3. Actions.form-actions: end-aligned, --space-3 apart, --space-4 above.

Form layout has no sizes; buttons and fields inside it carry their own.

  • Invalid: on submit, client checks run first; the summary appears at the top, takes focus, and each field shows its own error. Values stay as typed.
  • Submitting: the pressed button is disabled aria-busy="true"; its neighbours are disabled; text fields and textareas are readonly; radios, swatches and chips are disabled. Nothing moves, so there is no layout shift.
  • Failed (the server could not be reached) is not a form-summary: it is an alert such as "The book wasn’t removed", with the form returned to its default state.

Responsive behaviour

  • .form-actions wraps: at 360px "Not yet" and "I’m done!" share a line; if they ever do not fit, the primary wraps below and stays at the end.
  • .danger-zone wraps: on phones the button drops below the explanation; when there is room they sit side by side.
  • Forms are one column. Add a book puts the form in .span-8 and the cover preview in .span-4; both are full width until 1024px (D5).
  • The error summary is always the first thing in the form, so it is in view after focus moves to it on any screen size.

Theming

The summary inherits the danger alert's tokens (--color-danger-bg, --color-danger-fg, --color-danger-icon) and adds a --space-1 wide --color-danger-solid left edge. The danger zone uses --color-danger-border and --radius-lg on the page background, so it reads as a warning without filling with red.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Native <form> with novalidate so Shelfie's own messages replace the browser bubbles. The summary is role="alert" with tabindex="-1"; it is announced and receives focus after a failed submit (WCAG 3.3.1). The danger zone is a <section> named by its heading.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Enter in a text fieldSubmits the form.
Tab after a failed submitFocus is on the summary; Tab reaches its first link.
Enter on a summary linkMoves focus to that field, which is scrolled into view below the sticky top bar.

Focus

After a failed submit, focus moves to the summary. While submitting, focus stays put: read-only fields keep it; the busy button is disabled, so when the request ends focus goes to the result (the success toast, the summary, or the next page). In the danger zone, the button opens a dialog whose initial focus is the safe button.

Labelling

The summary title counts the problems ("Fix 2 things"); each link names the problem, not just the field. Field errors are joined to their controls with aria-describedby. A busy button keeps its name and adds aria-busy="true".

Motion

None of its own. The summary appears without animation; the busy button's spinner follows the button rules.

Touch

Summary links are inline text links in a list, with the list's line height giving at least 24px per row. Action buttons follow the button sizes.

Content

  • Summary titles are short and calm: "One thing to fix", "Fix 2 things", "We couldn’t sign you in". For Nia: "One more thing".
  • Summary links name the problem: "The book needs a title", "Question 1 needs an answer". The field error below says how to fix it.
  • The danger zone heading names the action ("Remove this book") and the text names what goes with it ("Deletes Nia’s 11 logged chapters and answers for it too.").
  • Primary labels say what saving does: "Save changes", "Save settings", "Add to my shelf". Not "Submit".
