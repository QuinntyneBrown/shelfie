Content

Short, warm words for a 9-year-old reader and brief, useful ones for her dad, in sentence case, with real verbs and no blame.

Overview

Shelfie talks to two people. Nia is 9 and reads every word on the screen herself, so her copy is short, warm and proud of her. Dad is skimming between other things, so his copy is brief and gets him to the next action. Both get the same plain-English rules: sentence case, real verbs, specific numbers, no blame.

Lift copy from the mocks before writing new copy; the microcopy library below is the starting point. The pattern page Content and tone shows these rules applied to whole flows.

Sentence case and punctuation

  • Sentence case everywhere: titles, buttons, labels, menu items, toasts. Only the first word and names are capitalised: "Add a question", "Give the reward", "The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe".
  • Chapter is capitalised with its number: "Chapter 12".
  • Curly quotes and apostrophes in product copy: ’ “ ”. Book titles in running text are plain; in a dialog title they go in quotes: Remove “The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe”?
  • No full stop on titles, labels, buttons or single-phrase toasts; full stops on sentences in help, error and toast text.
  • Middle dot separates short facts on one line: "Yesterday · Father Christmas came and gave everyone presents.".
  • Contractions are fine and friendlier: "didn’t", "you’re", "can’t".
  • "E-mail" with a hyphen; "sign in" (verb) and "sign-in" (noun).

Error messages

Formula: what happened (or what to do), why if it helps, and that nothing is lost whenever that is true. Never blame her, never say "invalid", "error" or "failed" on their own, and always offer the way forward.

WhereFormulaExample
Field errorWhat to do, then why or how (one or two short sentences)"Enter 11 or more. Nia has already logged 11 chapters."
Error summary"Fix N things" / "One thing to fix", then a linked list"Fix 2 things"
Failed action (dialog, toast)What did not happen + why + that nothing is lost"The book wasn’t removed" · "Shelfie couldn’t reach the server. Nothing has changed; try again in a moment."
Failed load"[Thing] didn’t load" + reassurance + Try again"Your shelf didn’t load" · "Your books and answers are safe; try again in a moment."
Wrong username or passwordOne generic sentence, never saying which was wrong"Username or password is wrong."
Short formsSay the fix in a few words"Enter a valid e-mail." · "At least 8 characters." · "That username is taken."
Server errorNot your fault + nothing lost + code to mention"Nothing you did caused this and nothing was lost. Try again in a moment. If it keeps happening, mention code SHF-4F2A."

Usage

  • Use the names from the household: "Nia", "Dad". Never "the user", "the child" or "the parent".
  • Talk about books and chapters, not "items" or "entries".
  • Celebrate effort, not correctness: "There are no wrong answers!"
  • Keep a toast to a title plus one sentence; keep a field error to two short sentences.
  • Write the accessible name as carefully as the visible text; read it aloud.

Accessibility

  • Plain language at a 9-year-old's reading level on her screens; short sentences help everyone.
  • Link and button text stands alone (2.4.4): "Review Chapter 10", not "Click here".
  • Emoji are decoration; the sentence must make sense without them, and sticker names are spelled out where a screen reader meets them ("Dad gave you a Star").
  • Counts in accessible names say what they count: "2 chapters to review", not "2".
